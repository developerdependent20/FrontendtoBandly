# Market size and customer profile: worship/church and band playback tools, Colombia and Latam

Research date: 2026-10-27. About 14 tool calls. Many searches returned thin results, so there are large gaps. Everything is labeled VERIFIED (from a fetched or cited source) or ESTIMATE/INFERENCE. Please do not treat any inference as data.

## How big is the addressable market in Colombia (iglesias evangélicas/cristianas, bandas) and Latam?

### Takeaway
Protestants and evangelicals are a large and growing share of adults: Pew puts Protestants at 15% in Colombia, 9% in Mexico, 18% in Peru, 19% in Chile and 16% in Argentina (2024). I found no reliable current count of evangelical churches in Colombia. The figures that exist are old or mix in "ministries" and "cultos". The count of bands, DJs and academies is a complete gap.

### Cited Findings
- Pew's 2024 survey (published 2026-01-21; more than 6,200 adults in 6 countries) gives these Protestant shares: Brazil 29%, Chile 19%, Peru 18%, Argentina 16%, Colombia 15%, Mexico 9%. Catholic shares are 46%, 46%, 67%, 58%, 60% and 67% respectively. These are per-country figures relayed by a secondary outlet; I could not open the Pew page itself (HTTP 403). — [Angelus News summary of Pew](https://angelusnews.com/news/world/pew-religion-latin-america/); primary: [Pew report PDF](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/01/PR_2026.01.21_religion-in-latin-america_report.pdf)
- Catholic share fell by at least 9 points in all six countries over the past decade. In Colombia, Mexico, Argentina and Chile there are now more religiously unaffiliated adults than Protestants. — [Pew via search summary](https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2026/01/21/catholicism-has-declined-in-latin-america-over-the-past-decade/)
- Pew also says Protestants are more likely than Catholics or the unaffiliated to say religion is very important to them and to attend weekly services. — [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2026/01/21/religious-and-spiritual-practices-in-latin-america/)
- The unaffiliated share rose over the decade: Colombia 23%, Chile 33%, Argentina 24%, Mexico 20%, Peru 12%. This is not directly about evangelicals but shows how fast religious composition is shifting. — [Baptist News Global citing Pew 2024](https://baptistnews.com/article/why-fewer-latin-americans-claim-religion-but-still-pray-and-believe/)
- Colombia, people: roughly 10 million evangelicals, from a 2016 media figure, versus about 5 million Protestants in a 2013 academic estimate. These two disagree by 2x. — [Las2orillas](https://www.las2orillas.co/?p=170195); [La Nación (2016)](https://www.nacion.com/revista-dominical/conquista-evangelica-de-la-fe-al-poder/ERK6Z6LL6ZA6VKA52ODO2G7KMM/story/)
- Colombia, churches: CEDECOL stated in 2006 that about 8,000 evangelical churches exist, with over 70% under its representation. This is nearly 20 years old. — [Global Ministries, Colombia Peace Summit 2006](https://www.globalministries.org/colombia_peace_summit_san_andr_10_10_2014_116/)
- Colombia, registry: a press article says the Ministerio del Interior registry holds around 100,000 "iglesias, denominaciones, cultos y ministerios" other than the Catholic church. It also says branches are not registered separately, so the real number of sites is higher. This counts legal entities, not congregations that could be buyers; date unclear. — [Las2orillas](https://www.las2orillas.co/?p=732439)
- The Ministerio del Interior keeps the public registry of religious entities and issues existence certificates (Decreto 505 de 2003; certificates valid one year per Decreto 1535 de 2015). The registry is the best primary source for a count, but I could not retrieve any statistics from it. — [Función Pública, Decreto 505/2003](https://www.funcionpublica.gov.co/eva/gestornormativo/norma.php?i=8276)

### Inferences
- ESTIMATE (mine, not sourced): Colombia has roughly 50 to 52 million people. Taking Pew's 15% Protestant share gives about 6 to 8 million adults. This is consistent with the 5 to 10 million media range.
- ESTIMATE: The commonly cited 8,000 (2006) and 100,000 (registry, mixed types) bracket the real number of evangelical congregations, so likely tens of thousands. Many are very small home or storefront churches that would not buy software. A realistic addressable subset (churches with a band and sound system) may be a fraction of that. The size of that subset needs validation.
- Latam scale: applying Pew shares to national populations gives tens of millions of Protestants. The largest are Brazil (out of scope), then Mexico (large population, low share), Colombia, Peru, Chile and Argentina.
- The Catholic "grupos de alabanza" segment (Renovación Carismática) is plausible but I found no data on it.

### Gaps
- Official or recent count of evangelical/cristiana churches in Colombia and other countries (the Ministerio del Interior registry was not retrievable).
- Number of wedding/event bands, DJs and music academies in Colombia or Latam: no sources found.
- Central America figures (Guatemala, for example, is widely reported as highly evangelical) were not searched or verified.
- Pew exact per-country Protestant numbers were not verified from the primary page (403).

## What price points are acceptable (COP/USD per month)?

### Takeaway
Reference prices exist for the main competitor (MultiTracks) in USD, and for consumer subscriptions in Colombia in COP. No survey on willingness to pay for worship or band tools in Latam was found.

### Cited Findings
- MultiTracks US price list (monthly, USD): Playback Premium $9.99; Playback Team Sharing $1.99; ChartBuilder $1.99; Chart Pro $14.99; Cloud Pro $10.99 (up to 500 GB); Stage Display add-on $8.99; RehearsalMix $2.99/seat; Rehearse Bundle $34.99 (team of 5); Live Bundle from $29.99; Playback Rentals $53.99; Credit Membership $49; MultiTracks One $134.99 or $169.33 (unclear). Church Streaming License $17.99; Church Lyric License $8.99. Playback Intro is free. — [MultiTracks pricing](https://www.multitracks.com/pricing)
- MultiTracks launched Secuencias.com in Spanish. The company says "secuencias" is the most common term in Latin countries. Playback has a Premium plan and a Live plan, and setlists import from Planning Center. — [MultiTracks blog](https://www.multitracks.com/blog/now-introducing-secuencias-com); [Secuencias.com comparison](https://www.secuencias.com/products/playback/comparison/primevsplayback)
- Independent Spanish producers sell per-song or per-pack multitracks (for example, from $3 per song) on Gumroad, with WhatsApp as the sales contact. This shows a low-priced one-off market. — [JC Música on Gumroad](https://jcmusica.gumroad.com/l/jubilosplit)
- Planning Center Services: sources conflict. One lists from $15/month for 20 team members, and the free tier covers 5 team members. Aggregator data, so low reliability. — [Planning Center pricing](https://www.planningcenter.com/pricing); [G2](https://g2.com/products/planning-center-services/pricing)
- Colombian consumer subscription reference points (COP, undated, from a Picodi comparison): Netflix 26,900; Prime Video 17,900; Spotify 14,900 (since raised to 16,900; student plan 9,300). — [Enter.co](https://www.enter.co/colombia/cuanto-gastan-los-colombianos-en-plataformas-de-streaming); [Cambio Colombia](https://cambiocolombia.com/tecnologia/articulo/2023/7/plataformas-digitales-estan-subiendo-los-precios-por-que)
- About 50% of surveyed Colombians reported sharing streaming accounts, and 8% cancelled after crackdowns. This hints at price sensitivity. — [Portafolio](https://www.portafolio.co/economia/finanzas/plataformas-de-streaming-en-colombia-usuarios-se-quejan-de-los-altos-costos-604673)

### Inferences
- ESTIMATE, unvalidated hypothesis only: an individual musician tier near COP 15,000 to 40,000 per month (about USD 4 to 10) anchors to Spotify/Netflix prices and MultiTracks Playback Premium at $9.99. A church or team tier of about USD 15 to 35 matches MultiTracks bundles but is likely high for small Colombian churches. Both need testing with real users.
- USD pricing is probably a barrier for local card payment for many users, so a COP price via local methods likely converts better. This is reasoning, not data.
- Per-song purchase at $3 shows many Latam users prefer one-off buys over subscriptions. Also reasoning.

### Gaps
- No survey of Latam church tech budgets, or of willingness to pay for subscriptions in COP vs USD.
- No data on what churches actually spend on sound or tech.
- Current MultiTracks pricing in COP or localized regional pricing was not found.

## What are the main purchasing channels and decision makers (pastor, director de alabanza, ingeniero de sonido)? Also payments, adoption, piracy and pains.

### Takeaway
No source directly documents the buyer persona. Payment infrastructure is well documented: bank transfers (PSE) and wallets (Nequi) dominate Colombian online payments. Digital access is broad but uneven. Piracy and pain points are not documented in any source I found.

### Cited Findings
- Payments, Colombia (Global Payments Report via Portafolio, Valora Analitik, El País): wallets were 29% of online transaction value in 2025, second after account-to-account transfers (including PSE), forecast at 41% of e-commerce value by 2030. Nequi is recognized by 54% of online shoppers. Cash use falls about 1% per year. Bre-B instant payment system (Banco de la República) launched in 2025. — [Portafolio](https://www.portafolio.co/tecnologia/bre-b-y-billeteras-impulsan-el-crecimiento-de-los-pagos-digitales-en-colombia-dice-el-global-payments-report-495647); [Valora Analitik](https://www.valoraanalitik.com/uso-de-efectivo-podria-disminuir-en-1-cada-ano-y-los-pagos-a-traves-de-billeteras-digitales-seguiran-en-aumento/)
- Digital access, Colombia: 65.6% of households had internet in 2024 (up from 54.2% in 2018), second-hand figure citing the DANE dashboard. More than 90% of people aged 5+ use a cell phone as the main access device. Rural gap is over 20 points. Mobile internet reached 86 per 100 people per MinTIC data. — [Impacto TIC](https://impactotic.co/tecnologia/uso-de-las-tic-en-colombia/); [Portafolio](https://www.portafolio.co/innovacion/solo-tres-de-cada-10-hogares-rurales-tiene-acceso-a-internet-564541)
- 96.3% of Colombian households owned a cell phone in 2022 (DANE). — [Valora Analitik](https://www.valoraanalitik.com/internet-en-colombia-en-2022-el-59-de-hogares-aumentaron-su-conexion/)
- Distribution via WhatsApp: indie multitrack sellers use WhatsApp as the sales and contact channel (see Gumroad link above). — [JC Música](https://jcmusica.gumroad.com/l/jubilosplit)

### Inferences
- ESTIMATE: Phone access is near universal, but the 65.6% household internet figure and rural gap imply an offline-capable app is important for churches, which often have poor Wi-Fi. This is inference.
- Typical decision structure is likely: the director de alabanza or musical director chooses the tool, the pastor approves spending, and the sound engineer influences hardware and workflow. This comes from general knowledge, not a cited source here; validate through interviews.
- Piracy and informal sharing (WhatsApp, Telegram, Drive) is widely assumed in the market but I found no source quantifying it. Netflix account-sharing data (50%) is only an indirect cultural signal.
- Payment implication: support PSE, Nequi and Bre-B plus cards, with a COP price. PayPal is relevant mainly for non-Colombian or USD buyers; I found no data on PayPal use.

### Gaps
- No data on who the buyer and decision maker is (pastor vs director de alabanza vs ingeniero de sonido). The searches returned nothing on this.
- No data on piracy or sharing of tracks via WhatsApp/Telegram/Drive.
- No data on pains (paper charts, sequences passed by WhatsApp, lack of light automation) from surveys or forums; these must be confirmed through direct interviews or Facebook/WhatsApp community research.
- No data for Mexico, Peru, Chile, Argentina or Central America on payments, internet reliability or church tech budgets (only Pew religion shares).
- No growth-trend data on the worship-tech market itself. Only the religious-composition trend (Catholic decline, rise of Protestant and unaffiliated) is available.
- Competitor Latam traction (user counts for Secuencias.com, Planning Center, etc.) not found.
